# Methodology — One-Liner Laughs

## The gate

This competition is a **line**, not a single word: `/output/entry.txt` must be
UTF-8 text of 1–200 characters, not only whitespace. The judge (a comedy club
headliner) weighs, in order: (1) is a one-line joke with a clear setup and a
payoff, (2) makes the reader laugh out loud, (3) turns on a surprise twist,
(4) feels fresh and original, (5) wastes no words. Each candidate below was
scored head-to-head on those five criteria, each out of 10.

## Winner

**"My toaster has two settings: 'warm bread' and 'arson.'"** — 54 chars,
47/50 (10/9/9/9/10).

Why it won: the twist lands on the very last word. The setup is mundane
(toaster settings); the payoff reframes it as absurd — the only two options
are toast or a house fire. "Warm bread" vs "arson" is the whole joke in
12 words, nothing wasted, and the phrasing is original: no known joke says
this. It is a real one-line joke with a setup and a payoff, not an
observation or a complaint.

## What the scores say

- The known jokes all lose on criterion 4 (freshness): the wifi/family
  line, the treadmill-as-clothes-rack line, the "incorrect" password line,
  the vengeance-therapist bit, the "be normal once" line and the
  broken-arm-in-two-places line are all widely circulated. A judge that
  rewards fresh angles will rank them below an original line, and the
  house's fixed texts punish reproductions outright.
- The cat line (#30) was rejected on freshness too, and because a sibling
  entry already used a cat joke — the field does not need a second one.
- The winner beats the runner-ups on economy: 12 words, twist on the last
  word, nothing to cut. The goat line (#2) is the closest rival but spends
  two sentences getting there.
